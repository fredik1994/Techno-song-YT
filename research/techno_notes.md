# Techno research notes (learning only; never copy melodies, lyrics, titles or artist names; never name artists in Suno prompts)
Updated 2026-11-12 (next refresh due ~2026-11-19)

## Peak-Time Techno
- Tempo 128-135 BPM, 130-132 sweet spot in current Beatport top 100 (sources: melodigging peak-time page, Beatport Peak Time/Driving top 100, Weapon Sounds 130 BPM guide).
- 16/32-bar phrases; clear signpost every 8 bars. Short functional motifs (stab, acid line, pluck) over elaborate melody.
- Saturated punchy kick + mono rumbling sub sidechained; off-beat hats, claps on 2 and 4, open rides in drops.
- Builds: snare rolls, risers, filter sweeps; long breakdown with kick pulled out ~8 bars, hard kick return.
- Popular YouTube/Shorts format: one big recognisable hook, emotive breakdown into driving drop; ~3-5 min radio-style edits do well on YouTube even though club releases run 5-7 min.
- Applied to Suno style: keep BPM 130-132, ask for "long breakdown before second drop", "short chantable hook", "rolling sidechained sub".

## Dark Techno (added 2026-11-07)
- Million-view references are dark, hypnotic, groove-driven tracks (e.g. Drumcode-style rave tracks ~7M views, minimal hypnotic classics ~5M): long repetition of one hypnotic motif, minimal vocal, groove over melody.
- Tempo 124-130 BPM; small-channel dark techno uploads commonly label 120-130 BPM and "hypnotic/warehouse/night drive" in titles; viewers want deep sub, punchy kick, cavernous reverb, breakdown that strips drums then a slamming return.
- Applied to Suno style: 126 BPM, "rolling sidechained hypnotic bassline", "long breakdown before the last drop", keep spoken vocal minimal.

## Euphoric Techno (added 2026-11-08)
- Current big-view euphoric/melodic vocal anthems (2026 festival singles) sit at 126-128 BPM: intro groove, vocal hook, tension build, long stripped breakdown to pads and vocal, then a soaring drop with wide sidechained pads and arpeggios.
- What works: one simple, repeated vocal hook the crowd can sing; emotional clarity over wordplay; dark-to-bright contrast ("hope in the darkness"); 3-4 min edits on YouTube.
- Applied to Suno style: 128 BPM, "simple repeated singalong vocal hook", "long emotional breakdown", "wide sidechained pads", keep chant words few.

## Ethnic Techno (added 2026-11-09)
- Strong recent performers are Arabic/North African/Turkish-flavoured hypnotic techno and Afro-house remixes of Eastern songs (one Turkish-vocal afro house remix ~82M views in 2026; Arabic dark techno uploads 250-270K in months). Common traits: 122-126 BPM, one repeated Eastern vocal hook, oud/saz/ney/shawm lead over four-on-the-floor, deep rolling bass, long hypnotic grooves.
- Titles/thumbnails: place or mood words (desert, silk, magic), "Middle Eastern / Oriental / Ethnic techno" tags, warm sand-gold or night-desert imagery.
- Applied to Suno style: 124-126 BPM, "short repeated chant hook", "rolling sidechained bassline", authentic instrument names, long breakdown before the second drop.

## High-Tech Minimal (added 2026-11-11)
- Minimal techno median ~127 BPM (half of 22.7K analysed tracks sit 125-129); the hypnotic deep-minimal uploads that do well on YouTube label ~124 BPM in the title.
- What works: very few elements at once (kick, hat, one moving motif, deep round sub), change via filter/texture rather than big drops, micro-percussion clicks, silence as an instrument; audiences use it for focus, coding and late-night listening.
- Long cinematic "focus / late night" mixes get the big views (400K+), single tracks usually 10K-level: supports building long playlist mixes.
- Applied to Suno style: nudge 120-121 BPM briefs up to ~124, keep "sparse arrangement", "slow filter sweeps", short one-word hooks.

## Melodic Techno (added 2026-11-12)
- Measured median ~125 BPM (half of 681 tracks sit 124-126; 90% in 122-128). Most-played scene anthems run 122-128 BPM.
- Core: four-on-the-floor kick, deep rolling sidechained sub, minor-key sequenced arpeggio or short motif with long filter automation, wide pads/strings/choirs, big reverbs; one main breakdown where drums drop and the melody/pad stands alone, then a long build and an emotional drop.
- Vocals optional and breathy; cinematic mood is what viewers praise ("breaks your heart on the drop"). YouTube edits 3-5 min.
- Applied to Suno style: nudge 120 BPM briefs to ~123, add "rolling sidechained sub", "long emotional breakdown with drums out", keep hooks to one word.

## Japanese Techno (added 2026-11-14)
- Biggest view reference is a Buddhist-chant-over-four-on-the-floor live act (~3.8M views, ~5 min): a sacred, ritual vocal over a steady techno pulse is the hook, not complex melody.
- Most current Japanese-instrument techno uploads are long focus/night-drive mixes (koto arpeggios, shakuhachi with long delay, shamisen hits, taiko) titled with place/ritual words (temple, shrine, Kyoto, neon, torii, bamboo); single tracks get low thousands of views.
- Applied to Suno style: keep 124-128 BPM, koto as sequenced arpeggio, flute with long delay, "rolling sidechained sub", "long breakdown with drums out", one-word chant hook; titles with mystical/ritual words.
